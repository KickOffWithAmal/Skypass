What is the Australia Subclass 600 Visitor Visa?

The Australia Subclass 600 Visitor Visa (Tourist Stream) is an e-visa that allows Indian passport holders to visit Australia for tourism, holidays, visiting family or friends, or short business trips. The visa is applied for online via the Australian Department of Home Affairs' ImmiAccount portal.

The Subclass 600 is typically valid for up to 1 year with multiple entries. Each stay is generally limited to 3 months, though longer stays can be requested.

Visa decisions are made solely by the Australian Department of Home Affairs. Approval cannot be guaranteed.

Who Can Apply?

Indian passport holders who wish to visit Australia for tourism, visiting family or friends, or short business activities may apply. Applicants must demonstrate sufficient funds, genuine tourist intent, and strong ties to India showing intention to return home.

Documents Required

Key documents for an Australia Subclass 600 visa from India typically include:

  • Valid passport with at least 6 months validity beyond intended stay
  • Completed online application via ImmiAccount
  • Recent passport-sized photographs
  • Bank statements for the last 6 months showing sufficient funds
  • Income tax returns for the last 3 years
  • Employment letter with salary, NOC, and leave approval
  • Confirmed flight itinerary and hotel bookings
  • Evidence of ties to India (family, employment, property)

Requirements may change. Always verify with the Australian Department of Home Affairs.

Application Process

  1. Create an ImmiAccount on the Australian Department of Home Affairs website.
  2. Complete and submit the Subclass 600 online application.
  3. Pay the visa application charge (currently AUD 190 as of 2024, subject to change).
  4. Upload all supporting documents as required.
  5. Wait for the Department of Home Affairs to process and grant the e-visa.

Processing Time

Skypass Visa Services completes documentation preparation within a maximum of 5 working days. The Australian Department of Home Affairs typically processes Subclass 600 applications within 20 to 40 calendar days. Processing times vary based on application volume and completeness.

Common Reasons for Refusal

Australia Subclass 600 applications are frequently refused when the Department is not satisfied of genuine visitor intent. Common reasons include:

  • Insufficient evidence of financial capacity
  • Weak ties to India — inadequate proof of employment, family, or property
  • Vague or inconsistent travel purpose
  • Incomplete document submission
  • Previous visa refusals or immigration compliance issues

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about Australia visa services.

What is the Australia Tourist Visa (Subclass 600)?
The Australia Visitor Visa (Subclass 600) Tourist stream allows Indian passport holders to visit Australia for tourism, visiting family or friends, or recreational activities. The application is submitted online through the Australian Department of Home Affairs' ImmiAccount portal. It is an eVisa — once granted, it is electronically linked to your passport with no sticker. The visa can be granted for 3, 6, or 12 months depending on the application and assessment.
What documents are required for Australia tourist visa from Kerala?
Key documents include: valid passport, completed online ImmiAccount application, recent passport photographs meeting Department of Home Affairs specs, bank statements for 3–6 months, employment certificate with salary confirmation, Income Tax Returns, confirmed return flight tickets, accommodation bookings, travel insurance, and evidence of ties to India (property, family). Retired applicants need pension statements or investment documents.
How long does Australia tourist visa processing take from India?
Australia visitor visa processing times vary significantly. The Department of Home Affairs processes 75% of Tourist stream applications within 29 days and 90% within 48 days, but individual times can be shorter or longer. Skypass completes document preparation in up to 5 working days. Apply at least 6–8 weeks before your travel date.
What is the Australia tourist visa fee for Indians?
The base application fee for the Australia Subclass 600 Tourist visa is AUD 190 (approximately ₹10,000–₹11,000). This is paid online to the Department of Home Affairs. There are no VFS charges for the standard online application. Additional fees may apply if health checks or further assessments are required. Skypass charges a separate consultancy fee.
Do I need a health examination for an Australia visitor visa?
Most short-term visitor visa applicants from India are not required to undergo a health examination. However, the Department of Home Affairs may request a health check in certain cases — for example, if you plan to stay longer than 3 months, or if you have worked in certain health or aged care settings. Skypass will advise if a health check applies to your profile based on the application.
What financial proof do I need for an Australia visitor visa?
There is no fixed minimum bank balance specified by the Department of Home Affairs. You need to demonstrate sufficient funds to cover your costs during the visit (flights, accommodation, daily expenses) and to return to India. Bank statements for 3–6 months showing consistent activity are more persuasive than a single large recent deposit. Salary slips, ITR, and property ownership all help demonstrate financial stability.
Can my parents visit Australia on a tourist visa?
Yes, retired parents can apply for an Australia Subclass 600 Tourist visa. The key factors for retired applicants are: pension or savings income, owned property in India, strong family ties, and a credible reason to return. If a child in Australia is sponsoring the visit, an invitation letter with the sponsor's immigration status documents and financial capacity is important. Skypass can help retired applicants build a strong file.
What are common reasons for Australia visitor visa refusal?
Common refusal reasons include: insufficient evidence that you will leave Australia before the visa expires, inadequate funds relative to the trip cost, previous immigration breaches or overstays in Australia or other countries, health or character issues, and inconsistencies between the application and supporting documents.
